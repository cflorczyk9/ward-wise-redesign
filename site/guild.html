The member charter.

Six rules every member signs, so money can never change what residents see.

  1. The public site stays free

    Anything a member can see, a resident can see. Members pay for help and custom work, never for private numbers.

  2. Money never moves a number

    Members can request new measures. They cannot change, hide or reweight existing ones, including ones that reflect poorly on them.

  3. Residents keep half the queue

    Half of each quarter's new measures come from resident nominations. Members vote on the other half.

  4. No data about individuals

    Ward Wise measures places. It never sells to surveillance or policing tools and never tracks who views what.

  5. No campaigns, no elected officials

    Candidates, campaigns, parties and aldermanic offices cannot join or buy analysis. Consulting firms can join at the published price, open equally to every party.

  6. Open books every quarter

    Dues received, money spent and the full member list are published line by line.

Governance.

A five-seat council meets each quarter at Chi Hack Night. Members elect two seats, the volunteer team holds two, and one goes to a resident chosen from that year's nominators.

Proposed as a nonprofit, starting under a fiscal sponsor. The team has not settled this yet.

Questions.

Why don't residents pay?

Residents supply the nominations, photos and block audits that make the data worth using. Of the 42 comparable projects we studied, almost none charge residents, and the ones that last charge organizations.

What if our area looks bad in the data?

The number stays. The charter bars any member from changing or hiding a measure. You can ask for context in the data notes or nominate a measure you think is missing.

Can a ward office join?

No. Ward Wise ranks every ward, so aldermanic offices can't be members or buy analysis. Every alderperson sees the same free report card as everyone else. City and county agencies can join at the published price.

What if we can't afford the dues?

Select that option in the price finder and pay what you can. Nobody is turned away for money.

Where does the model come from?

Canada's Community Data Program has pooled member fees to buy and share local data since 2010. In the UK, mySociety keeps its public sites free and sells a supported version to councils. The Guild draws on both.

Is Ward Wise a nonprofit?

That is the proposal. It would start under a fiscal sponsor, a charity that holds the money, and apply for its own nonprofit status later.

Keep Chicago's data public.

The application takes about three minutes. Dues begin only after you have tried a view built for you.
