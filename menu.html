Ward by ward

All fifty wards, and how each office spends its menu money.

Chicago gives each ward's aldermanic office about $1.5 million a year for capital projects in the ward, covering street resurfacing, lighting, sidewalks, alleys, parks, cameras, schools, and bike and pedestrian work. Each office decides how to split that money across those categories, and some let residents vote on part of it through participatory budgeting. The mix on this page is a choice made inside that office, not a description of the ward it serves.

How to read this

What each percent measures

Every figure here is a share of the ward's menu budget, the portion spent on that category. Ward Wise Penlight doesn't publish a project by project dollar breakdown, so shares are what the data can show.

Whose decision this is

The mix of categories below is decided inside the ward's aldermanic office. It doesn't describe the neighborhood's needs, wealth, or population, only how one office chose to allocate its budget.

What "budget used" means

Budget used is the share of the year's menu allotment that actually got committed to projects. A lower number means part of the budget sat unspent that year. It says nothing about how much the ward was allotted to start.

Where the numbers come from

Every measure is defined in the Ward Wise Penlight dictionary, which spells out exactly what each one counts and how it's built.
